💥 20th Century History - Empire, War, and Rebirth
Japan's most turbulent century: from imperial expansion and devastating war to economic miracle and pacifist constitution. A century that transformed Japan from feudal backwater to modern superpower.
Meiji Modernization 明治維新 (1868-1912)
Westernization: Government-led modernization - army, navy, constitution, education system. Imperial Expansion: Sino-Japanese War (1894-1895), Russo-Japanese War (1904-1905). Economic Growth: GDP increases 15x. Zaibatsu conglomerates emerge. Legacy: Transforms Japan from feudal to modern state in one generation.
Taisho Democracy 大正民主 (1912-1926)
Democracy Movement: Universal male suffrage (1925). Political party governments. Economic Boom: Post-WWI prosperity. Heavy industry grows. Cultural Flourishing: "Taisho Democracy" - liberalism, women's rights, proletarian literature. Earthquake: 1923 Great Kanto Earthquake kills 140,000, destroys Tokyo/Yokohama.
Rise of Militarism 軍国主義の台頭 (1926-1941)
Army Dominates: Assassinations of politicians, military budget control. Manchuria Invasion: 1931 - start of aggressive expansion. China War: 1937 - full-scale invasion, Nanjing Massacre. Thought Control: Peace Preservation Law, suppression of dissent. Oil Embargo: 1941 US embargo forces decision for war.
Pacific War 太平洋戦争 (1941-1945) - Japan's Total War
Pearl Harbor Attack (December 7, 1941): Simultaneous strikes on Pearl Harbor, Philippines, Malaya, Singapore, Hong Kong. US Pacific Fleet crippled, 2,403 Americans killed. Yamamoto Isoroku planned the attack to buy time for conquest of Southeast Asia.

Early Victories (1941-1942): - Philippines: General Douglas MacArthur retreats to Bataan. Japanese take Manila in January 1942. - Singapore: "Impregnable fortress" falls in 70 days. 80,000 British/Commonwealth troops captured. - Dutch East Indies: Oil-rich islands conquered for resources. Japanese control 90% of world's rubber. - Coral Sea (May 1942): First carrier-vs-carrier battle. Tactical Japanese victory, strategic US win.

Turning Point - Battle of Midway (June 1942): US intelligence breaks Japanese codes. Four Japanese carriers (Akagi, Kaga, Soryu, Hiryu) sunk. Yamamoto's offensive permanently crippled.

Island Hopping Campaign (1943-1944): - Guadalcanal: Six-month battle for control. 25,000 Japanese casualties. - Tarawa, Saipan, Guam: Intense banzai charges, civilian massacres. - Peleliu, Iwo Jima: Kamikaze attacks begin. 28,000 Japanese defenders killed on Iwo Jima.

Firebombing Campaign (1945): Curtis LeMay's B-29s conduct 330 air raids. - March 9-10, 1945: Tokyo firebombed. 100,000+ killed, 1 million homeless. More destructive than Hiroshima. - Other cities: Nagoya, Osaka, Kobe, Yokohama systematically destroyed. - Tokyo alone: 50% of city burned, 800,000+ dead across campaign.

Atomic Bombings: - Hiroshima (August 6): "Little Boy" uranium bomb. 140,000 immediate deaths, radiation deaths follow. - Nagasaki (August 9): "Fat Man" plutonium bomb. 74,000 immediate deaths. - Combined: 200,000+ dead from bombs and radiation. War crimes by any definition.

Soviet Invasion (August 9): USSR declares war, invades Manchuria. 10,000 Japanese soldiers killed, millions of civilians displaced.

Imperial Surrender (August 15, 1945): Emperor Hirohito broadcasts capitulation. First time Japanese hear his voice. "Jewel Voice Broadcast" ends war. Emperor cites atomic weapons but not defeat.

Human Cost: 2.7 million Japanese military/civilian deaths. Millions more in occupied territories. Total Asian deaths: 10-20 million including Chinese famine, Korean forced labor, comfort women.
American Occupation 連合国軍総司令部 (1945-1952)
Douglas MacArthur: Supreme Commander oversees democratization. Peace Constitution: Article 9 renounces war, military. Land Reform: Redistributes land from landlords to farmers. Zaibatsu Dissolution: Breaks up industrial conglomerates. War Crimes Trials: Tokyo Trials prosecute leaders. Educational Reforms: 6-3-3-4 system, coeducation.
Economic Miracle 経済の奇跡 (1950s-1980s)
Rapid Growth: 10% annual GDP growth for decades. Lifetime Employment: Job security system. Export-Driven: Shipbuilding, automobiles, electronics. Tokyo Olympics: 1964 - symbol of recovery. Heavy Industry: Steel, chemicals, machinery. Quality Control: Kaizen, just-in-time production.
Bubble Economy & Lost Decade バブル経済 (1980s-1990s)
Asset Bubble: 1980s stock and real estate speculation. Peak & Burst: 1990 - bubble collapses. Stagnation: "Lost Decade" - deflation, banking crisis. Structural Issues: Aging population, inflexible labor market. Failed Stimulus: Multiple government spending programs. Recovery Efforts: Ongoing attempts at reform.
🔄 Lasting Impact on Modern Japan
Pacifist Constitution: Article 9 shapes foreign policy, no military aggression.

Economic Model: Export-oriented, quality-focused manufacturing.

Social Cohesion: Post-war emphasis on harmony, community.

Technological Leadership: Recovery focused on innovation.

Global Relations: US alliance, economic partnerships, cultural soft power.

Historical Memory: Ongoing debate about war responsibility, education.
