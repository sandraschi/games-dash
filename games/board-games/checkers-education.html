📜 The Rules

Setup

• 8×8 board with dark squares on the left

• 12 pieces per player on dark squares only

• Red moves first

Movement

Regular Pieces:

• Move diagonally forward one square to empty dark square

• Cannot move backward (until crowned)

Kings (Crowned):

• Move diagonally forward OR backward

• Still only one square at a time (American rules)

• Crowned when reaching opposite end of board

Capturing

• Jump over opponent's piece diagonally

• Must capture if possible (forced capture rule)

• Multiple jumps in one turn allowed

• Captured pieces removed from board

Example: Red can jump black piece diagonally

Winning

✅ Capture all opponent's pieces

✅ Opponent has no legal moves

🤝 Draw if position repeats or no progress for 40 moves

🏛️ History & The Solved Game

Ancient Origins

• Played in ancient Egypt (circa 3000 BCE)

• Similar game found in Ur, Iraq (circa 3000 BCE)

• Modern rules established in France, 12th century

• Called "Draughts" in UK, "Checkers" in USA

🎯 Solved in 2007! (Chinook Program)

Dr. Jonathan Schaeffer and team at University of Alberta proved:

• Perfect play = Draw

• Neither player can force a win

• 500 billion billion positions analyzed

• Took 18 years of computation!

🏆 Chinook's Achievement

• First program to win world championship (1994)

• Beat Marion Tinsley (only lost 7 games in 45 years!)

• Tinsley withdrew due to illness, Chinook declared champion

• Chinook never lost a game after 1994

🧠 Strategy Guide

🎯 Opening Principles

✅ Control the center (squares 14, 15, 18, 19)

✅ Advance pieces together (don't isolate)

✅ Don't rush to king too early

❌ Don't move same piece twice in opening

👑 King Me Wisely

✅ King when it gains advantage

✅ Kings are mobile and powerful

⚠️ Don't sacrifice 2 pieces for 1 king

💡 2 kings vs 1 king = forced win

🎭 Tactical Patterns

The Fork: Attack 2 pieces at once

The Pin: Trap piece against board edge

The Bridge: Create escape route for king

The Sacrifice: Give up piece for better position

⚔️ Endgame Tips

✅ 2 pieces vs 1 = usually winning

✅ Trade when ahead in material

✅ Keep kings in center, not corners

💡 3 vs 2 = slow methodical win

🎓 Advanced Concepts

The Dog Hole: Square 7 (double corner)

• Most important square on the board!

• Control it to dominate your side

• Many openings fight for this square

The Bridge Position:

• Two pieces controlling diagonal

• Prevents opponent king from advancing

• Key defensive formation

The Forced Exchange:

• Setting up mandatory captures

• Forces opponent into bad position

• Plan 3-4 moves ahead

🌍 International Variants

🇬🇧 English Checkers (American)

• 8×8 board, 12 pieces each

• Kings move one square

• This is what we're playing!

🇫🇷 International Draughts

• 10×10 board, 20 pieces each

• Kings can "fly" (move multiple squares)

• Must capture maximum number possible

• More complex than English version

🇷🇺 Russian Checkers

• Regular pieces can capture backward

• Kings can fly

• Popular in Eastern Europe

🇮🇹 Italian Checkers

• Pieces cannot capture kings!

• No jumping backward even for kings

• Most restrictive variant

💡 Pro Tips

✅ DO

• Think 3-4 moves ahead

• Control the center

• Keep pieces together

• Look for forced captures

• Trade when ahead in material

• Crown pieces at right moment

❌ DON'T

• Leave pieces isolated and weak

• Rush pieces forward without support

• Ignore forced captures (illegal!)

• Trade when behind in material

• Move same piece repeatedly

• Neglect king safety in endgame
